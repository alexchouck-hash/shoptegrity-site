# The Distributed Company Town: How to Reclaim the Other 90%

> *"The company owned the mine, the company owned the store, the company owned the land, and the company wrote the laws. If you didn't like it, you were free to starve in the woods."*

## 1. The Modern Scrip

You do not live in a 19th-century coal patch in West Virginia. You are not forced to spend copper company scrip at an isolated wooden commissary. You have direct deposit, a debit card with a microchip, and the freedom to browse forty thousand items under the LED lights of a suburban supercenter or scroll millions more on a glass smartphone screen.

Yet the arithmetic of your life produces the exact same terminal result: **at the end of thirty days of relentless labor, zero net equity remains in your hands, while the entity that paid you has reclaimed both your wage and your productive time.**

Consider the closed extraction loop of the modern median household:

1. **You sell your labor:** You work 40 to 60 hours a week for an employer whose primary fiduciary duty—enforced by securities law and activist hedge funds—is to suppress labor costs to maximize return on invested capital (ROIC).
2. **You receive your wage:** A deposit arrives in your account on the 1st and the 15th.
3. **The immediate recapture begins:**
   * **Shelter (35–45% of net income):** Transferred to an institutional landlord, a mortgage serviced by a mega-bank, or a real estate investment trust (REIT) traded on Wall Street.
   * **Food & Essentials (15–20% of net income):** Transferred to one of four supermarket conglomerates. Behind those shelves, according to USDA data, the actual farmers and growers capture just **11.8 cents** of your dollar. The remaining **88.2 cents** is consumed by corporate packaging, corporate logistics, supermarket slotting fees, and financial overhead.
   * **Essential Services (10–15% of net income):** You call a local plumber, roofer, or veterinarian whose truck carries a friendly family name ("Smith & Sons, Serving You Since 1984"). Unbeknownst to you, Smith sold out three years ago to a private equity rollup platform based in Delaware. The rates are 40% higher; the technicians work on aggressive commission quotas; the profit is wired out of your county by 5:00 PM.
   * **Healthcare, Insurance & Telecom (15–20% of net income):** Paid directly to oligopolies with statutorily guaranteed margins.

By the 28th of the month, the wage has vanished. Where did it land?

According to Federal Reserve Survey of Consumer Finances data, the **top 10% of households own approximately 93% of all corporate equities and mutual funds**. The top 1% alone holds more stock wealth than the entire bottom 90% combined. 

The terminal destination of every transaction is mathematically identical. The dollars you earn from the corporate system do not circulate among your neighbors; they flow through you like water through a siphon, pooling into the asset ledgers of the top 10%.

This is not a free market of decentralized peers. It is a **Distributed Company Town**.

## 2. The Monopsony on Livelihood: Why They "Own the Jobs"

When people suggest that working-class and middle-class people should simply "work elsewhere" or "demand better wages," they misunderstand the structural consolidation of the job market.

Economists call this **monopsony**—a market condition where there are many sellers (workers offering labor) but only one or two dominant buyers (employers purchasing labor).

Over the last thirty years, corporate mergers and private equity rollups have eliminated independent employers in every sector:
* In agriculture, four meatpackers control over 80% of cattle processing.
* In pharmacy, three pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) control 80% of prescription volume.
* In local trades, national private equity aggregators have bought thousands of independent HVAC, plumbing, veterinary, dental, and collision-repair shops.

When three conglomerates own all the employers in an industry, they don't need to collude in a smoke-filled room to keep wages flat. They simply benchmark against each other's salary bands. 

**They own the jobs because they own the physical and digital rails of commerce:**
* They own the distribution warehouses and the intermodal shipping lanes.
* They own the payment terminals and swipe fees (2.5–3.5% extracted from every transaction).
* They own the search engines and social media ad auctions that charge independent makers an 80% customer-acquisition tax just to be seen.
* They have direct access to the Federal Reserve window and commercial paper markets, borrowing billions at near-sovereign rates while independent small businesses pay 11% to 24% on credit lines.

When the wealthy own the rails, they do not create jobs out of civic benevolence. They maintain a hostage economy: you must submit to their metrics, their arbitrary layoffs, and their surveillance tools because they have methodically eradicated the independent alternative.

## 3. The Camouflage Economy: The Illusion of Competition

Why haven't people revolted with their wallets? Because the modern corporate state understands something the 19th-century coal barons did not: **visible tyranny produces strikes; invisible consolidation produces compliance.**

Walk down any grocery aisle:
* You see fifty brands of laundry detergent with different fonts, pastel bottles, and promises of alpine freshness. Forty-one of them are manufactured by two companies: Procter & Gamble and Unilever.
* You pick an organic cereal featuring a hand-drawn barn, a smiling cow, and a story about family heritage. The parent company is General Mills or Nestlé.
* You take your dog to a veterinary hospital named after the local oak tree. The clinic was quietly acquired last year by Mars Inc. (the candy conglomerate that now controls over 2,500 veterinary hospitals worldwide).

The consumer believes they are casting an economic ballot every day. But the ballot has only one candidate printed under fifty different nicknames. 

When you spend your hard-earned dollar at what appears to be a quaint neighborhood bakery or natural apothecary, you are often merely feeding the same asset management balance sheets (BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street, Apollo, Blackstone) that hold the deed to your mortgage.

## 4. The Arithmetic of Freedom: The Horizontal Multiplier

How do you break a closed extraction loop without begging the owners of the loop for reform?

You change the direction of the arrow.

When a dollar is spent at a multinational corporate chain or a private equity rollup:
* **86 cents** leaves the local community immediately.
* It pays for executive stock options, corporate PR firms, corporate law retainers, debt-service payments on leveraged buyouts, and Wall Street dividends.
* Only **~14 cents** lingers in the community, almost entirely as minimum-wage or low-tier retail payroll.

When a dollar is spent with an **authentic, independent, worker-owned, or community-rooted business**:
* **68 cents** stays in the local community (Civic Economics Local Multiplier Studies).
* That dollar pays a local owner who buys groceries at a local market.
* That local business hires a local bookkeeper, a local web designer, a local sign painter, and banks at a local community credit union.
* The credit union takes that deposit and lends it to a young family down the street to start a carpentry business or insulate their home.

```
THE MULTIPLIER MATH: $10,000 SPENT LOCALLY VS. EXTRACTIVELY

EXTRACTIVE SPEND (National Chain / PE Rollup):
Round 1: $10,000 spent ──────> $8,600 exported to Wall Street / HQ
Round 2: $1,400 local wages ──> $1,204 exported to corporate retail
Round 3: $196 remaining ─────> Leaves the area
TOTAL LOCAL ECONOMIC ACTIVITY GENERATED: ~$11,600

HORIZONTAL PEER SPEND (Independent Producer / Co-op / Local):
Round 1: $10,000 spent ──────> $6,800 recirculated locally (B2B + wages)
Round 2: $6,800 ─────────────> $4,624 recirculated locally
Round 3: $4,624 ─────────────> $3,144 recirculated locally
Round 4: $3,144 ─────────────> $2,138 recirculated locally
TOTAL LOCAL ECONOMIC ACTIVITY GENERATED: ~$31,000+ (2.7x more wealth)
```

**Every 10% shift in consumer spending toward peer-owned independent businesses triples the net liquid wealth circulating among working families.**

It turns an arid desert into a lush watershed. And most importantly: **it creates jobs that no distant board of directors can offshore to boost their fourth-quarter bonus.**

## 5. The Blueprint: Building the Parallel Circuit

Dismantling the Distributed Company Town does not require an act of Congress, an armed insurrection, or a billionaire savior. It requires **disciplined, collective horizontal procurement**.

Here is the operational blueprint for the 90%:

### Pillar A: Remove the Camouflage (Radical Visibility)
You cannot vote with your dollar if the ballot is counterfeit. 
* Every consumer must be equipped with instant, unvarnished beneficial ownership data. When you stand at the counter or click "checkout," you must see:
  1. Who cashes the net dividend?
  2. Does this dollar circulate to a neighbor or exit through a Delaware shell corporation?
  3. Is there a peer-owned maker, grower, or provider within 15 miles?
* **Shoptegrity is built to be that lens.**

### Pillar B: The B2B Peer Sourcing Accord
Individual grocery shopping cannot carry the entire burden. The biggest pool of unexamined capital in any city is **Business-to-Business (B2B) spending**.
* If an independent coffee shop buys all its beans from a multinational commodity trader, its paper cups from an international chemical giant, and its tech stack from Silicon Valley monopolies, it is acting as an unpaid collection agent for Wall Street.
* Independent businesses must form local procurement circles: sourcing food, bookkeeping, trades, printing, cleaning, and maintenance exclusively from other independent operators.

### Pillar C: Capturing the "Silver Tsunami" (Worker Co-op Conversions)
Over the next decade, an estimated **2.9 million baby-boomer business owners** will retire, representing over $10 trillion in business value and 32 million jobs. 
* If we do nothing, private equity aggregators will buy these machine shops, HVAC outfits, lumber mills, and bakeries, strip their assets, and turn their workers into disposable labor.
* If we intervene, we can convert these retiring businesses into **Worker Cooperatives and Employee Stock Ownership Trusts (ESOPs)**. The retiring owner gets fair market value for their life's work; the employees become the owners; and the business is permanently inoculated against corporate extraction.

### Pillar D: Starving Wall Street's Weapon (The Credit Union Pivot)
Where do you keep your savings? If your emergency fund and checking account sit at Chase, Bank of America, or Wells Fargo, **your own deposits are being loaned to private equity firms to buy the company you work for and the apartment building you live in.**
* Move your money to a local Credit Union or Community Development Financial Institution (CDFI).
* Credit unions are member-owned mutuals. They do not have public shareholders. Their capital is legally bound to finance local homes, local small businesses, and community infrastructure.

### Pillar E: Defeating the "Poverty Penalty" Through Commoning
Conglomerates maintain power because their scale makes processed food and plastic goods cheap. Working families on tight margins cannot afford a "feel-good luxury tax."
* We must rebuild **practical collective purchasing**:
  * Bulk buying clubs direct from farmer cooperatives (cutting grocery costs by 35%).
  * Tool libraries and repair cafes (ending the need to buy disposable Chinese appliances from Big Box stores every two years).
  * Community gardens and seed-sharing networks.

## 6. The Verdict: We Are the Economy

The billionaire class did not invent food, shelter, warmth, medicine, or craftsmanship.
Working people grow the grain, turn the wrenches, lay the bricks, write the software, nurse the sick, and drive the trucks. 

The wealthy merely placed a tollbooth between the worker who makes the thing and the neighbor who needs it.

Every time we buy from a neighbor, hire an independent craftsperson, bank at a credit union, or turn an employer into a worker cooperative, we kick down a tollbooth. 

We do not need them to give us jobs.  
We do not need them to manufacture our goods.  
We only need to stop handing them our future thirty days at a time.

**Point your money at your neighbor, and watch it come back to you.**
