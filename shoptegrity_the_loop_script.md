# "The Loop": Shoptegrity Hero Animation Script

**Formats:** (A) 24-second silent looping gif/video for the site, (B) 45 to 60 second Kling film with voiceover.
**Audience:** everyday consumers. No jargon, no Sankey literacy required.
**One-line idea:** Your money is always in a loop. Right now the loop runs through you and out. Point it at your neighbors and it comes back.

---

## 1. Creative concept

The three existing gifs each expose one problem (hidden parent, disguised rollup, $100 split). This one is the *why it matters* piece that ties them together, so it belongs at the top of the home page.

The visual device is a single glowing coin that the viewer follows. We watch it travel two loops:

1. **The Drain Loop (red/amber):** paycheck → ad → checkout → friendly storefront → the storefront is a mask → money leaves town → neighborhood dims → you work more, think less, follow the next ad. Repeat. Each lap the neighborhood is a little darker.
2. **The Neighbor Loop (green):** paycheck → a lookup → a real person → that person spends at the next neighbor → the coin comes back around to you. Each lap the neighborhood is a little brighter.

The gif loops seamlessly, which is the point: the loop is the message. The final frame hands the coin back to the viewer.

**Tone guardrails (from your own methodology):**
- Show mechanisms, not villains. No cartoon fat cats. The "bad guy" is a facade and an arrow leaving the map.
- The viewer is never the fool. Frame it as "the ballot was hidden from you," not "you've been brainwashed." People who feel accused close the tab.
- Every number on screen must be one you can cite on /methodology. This script uses only two: the USDA 11.8¢ farm share and the Fed figure that the top 10% of households hold roughly 90% of stocks. Everything else is shown as motion, not as a number.

## 4. Tools and workflow

### Version A (the gif): build it in code, not in an AI video tool
Your existing gifs are clearly HTML-rendered cards, so keep the pipeline the same and deterministic.

1. **Claude Code** writes one self-contained `loop.html`: inline SVG neighborhood, coin motion along SVG paths, text swaps, all driven by a single timeline (GSAP timeline, or plain CSS keyframes plus the Web Animations API). Give it section 2 of this file as the spec and one of the existing gifs as the style reference.
2. **Antigravity** is the better place to iterate on look and timing, since its agent can open the page in the built-in browser, watch it, and adjust. Use it for polish passes ("shot 4 feels slow, tighten to 3 s").
3. **Capture:** have Claude Code write a Playwright script that steps the timeline frame by frame (seek the timeline, screenshot, repeat at 20 fps). Frame-stepping beats screen recording because it never drops frames.
4. **Encode:** `ffmpeg` for MP4/WebM, `gifski` for the gif (far better quality per byte than ffmpeg's gif encoder).
5. **Ship as video, fall back to gif.** A 24 s gif at 760 px will be 8 to 20 MB. Use `<video autoplay muted loop playsinline>` with WebM + MP4 sources on the site (well under 1 MB), and keep the gif for social posts and READMEs. Add `prefers-reduced-motion` handling that shows the final frame as a still.

### Version B (the film)
- **Kling** for shots 1 to 8 (image-to-video from hero stills).
- **ElevenLabs** (or your own voice) for the voiceover. A calm, plain, neighborly read. Not a movie-trailer voice.
- **CapCut or DaVinci Resolve** (free) for the cut, text overlays, the phone screen replacement in shot 6, and the logo card.
- Music: one simple acoustic or piano bed, licensed (Artlist, Epidemic) or CC0. It should turn from sparse to warm at shot 6.

### Suggested order
Build Version A first. It costs almost nothing, and the timing you settle on becomes the animatic for the Kling film.

## 5. Fixes to the existing gifs (noticed while reviewing)

- **shoptegrity_dollar.gif:** the captured frame shows "$0" for both totals and the row "Stays in Community: $100 only," which reads as a bug. Check the counter animation's end state.
- **Uncited numbers:** "Wall Street Buybacks $42 / Offshore Tax Havens $21 / Executive Bonuses $19" and "hiking rates 40%" are not figures your methodology can source, and "Offshore Tax Havens" is a characterization. Your own rule is "no evidence, no score." Either swap in citable figures (USDA Food Dollar, a specific company's 10-K buyback ratio, a Civic Economics recirculation study) or label the card clearly as an illustration. These gifs are the first thing a skeptic or a rated company's lawyer will screenshot.
- "Miller & Sons HVAC" is fictional, which is right. Add a tiny "illustrative example" tag so nobody thinks a real Miller & Sons is being accused.
